Transcript

ENGANO checks content against a brand's own rules before anything is published. It explains every problem, cites where the rule came from, and leaves the decision to people.

Employees, agencies and AI tools publish fast. Wrong facts, retired offers, off-brand tone and unreadable visuals slip out before anyone notices. ENGANO is the check that happens first.

A brand manager signs up and creates a brand. Creators choose a brand to write for.

Each workspace gets its own join code.

And its own rules. Here the manager writes a don't: never use the word cheap. ENGANO enforces it exactly.

Reviewers can't sign themselves up. The manager creates their account, and ENGANO generates the password.

The manager can also post an assignment with a deadline.

A creator signs up, then joins the workspace with the code the manager shared.

The studio is where drafts are checked. This one has a banned word, a wrong fact and a retired offer.

Specialised agents run at once: terminology, claims, tone, legal and accessibility. Problems are underlined in place.

Every finding names the rule or approved fact behind it. The manager's own rule is enforced here too.

Fixes are suggestions the creator accepts. Nothing is changed behind their back.

Problems remain, so this draft can't clear itself. It goes to a human reviewer.

Images are checked too. ENGANO reads the text in the picture, measures real contrast from the pixels, and notices when there is no description for screen readers.

A creator can paste a website address. ENGANO opens it in a real browser, scrolls the whole page and back, tests the links, tries a phone-sized screen and measures accessibility. This takes about a minute, so we skip ahead.

Where ENGANO can't see the site, or something is broken, it says what is wrong and what the site's owner must fix. It doesn't just suggest small edits.

The reviewer signs in with the account the manager created, and sees only their workspace's queue.

Each draft arrives with ENGANO's findings, their sources and suggested corrections.

They approve, or reject with a note the creator can act on.

A reviewer can add a WhatsApp number or Discord ID in settings, and review requests arrive there too.

The creator sees the reviewer's note, fixes the draft and resubmits.

This time nothing is left to fix. ENGANO clears it without bothering a reviewer.

The manager never reviews drafts. They watch the whole picture: where content stands, who sends what, what reviewers decided, and which problems keep repeating.

And every approved draft is here, read only.

That is the working product: three roles, a multi-agent review engine, image and website checks, and human control throughout. Still to build: revision history, brand library management for facts and terminology, and document import.
